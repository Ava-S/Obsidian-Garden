# What's a conceptual framework?

Writing for a Doctor of Philosophy (PhD): A guide to developing a string and coherent thesis.

A conceptual framework outlines the key concepts and their interrelationships - often visualized through a model - and is derived from the literature, prior research and the researcher's understanding of the phenomenon.

A conceptual framework emerges from critically engaging with literature and previous studies to map out how key ideas relate to each other in the context of the research problem.

This framework is unique to each study and helps delineate the scope and focus of inquiry.

# What are possible aims of a theory?

I found this interesting paper on The Nature of Theory in Information Systems, which presents that a theory - which is used rather broadly to encompass what might be termed elsewhere conjectures, models, frameworks or body of knowledge - in Information System can belong to one of five classes.

1. Theory for Analyzing: says what is,  
    The theory does not extend beyond analysis and description. No causal relationships among phenomena are specified and no predictions are made
2. Theory for Explaining: says what is, how, why, when and where.  
    The theory provides explanations but does not aim to predict with any precision. There are no testable propositions.
3. Theory for Predicting: says what is and what will be.  
    The theory provides predictions and has testable propositions, but does not have well-developed justificatory causal explanations.
4. Theory for Explaining and Predicting:  says what is, how, why, when, where and what will be.  
    The theory provides predictions and has both testable propositions and causal explanations
5. Theory for Design and Action: says how to do something  
    The theory gives explicit prescriptions (e.g. methods, techniques, principles of form and function) for constructing an artifact.

I think the conceptual framework will probably fall into the theory for design and action. We want to explain to others how to build and model object-centric event data. In the paper, they also mentioned that the theory for design and action closely aligns with design science. So I will read more on this tomorrow.

# Nature and Purpose of Conceptual Frameworks in Design Science

I read this paper today. Then define conceptual frameworks as constructs developed by researchers to represent sets of concepts and relationships that express some elements of their research.

Represent refers to both thinking about something and communicating that thinking to the rest

Concept and relationship refer to bits of structured knowledge

Construct refers to how concepts and relationships are integrated.

They've done an extensive literature study to investigate the nature of purpose of Conceptual Frameworks in Design Science Research.

They found several dimensions along which Conceptual Frameworks can be classified and they also provide a decision diagram in the end to determine what the purpose of your Conceptual Framework is.

They've identified the following dimensions

1. Stages in the DSR lifecycle
	1. Construction --> initial stage where researcher seeks to identify opportunities and gaps for contributing to knowledge
	2. Formulation --> the stage where the researcher defines the goals and the specific research question
	3. Answer --> the stage where the researches conveys the outcomes of the research

2. Views of the researcher about the phenomenon of interest
	1. Meta-view --> how to conduct DSR
	2. Generalized view --> the design of generalized solutions
	3. Specific view --> the design of specific solutions
3. Dimensions of research
	1. Way of knowing --> Position the research in relation to the knowledge base
		1. How it relates to the existing knowledge base,
		2. How it contributes new knowledge
		3. The methods of knowledge inquiry
	2. Way of framing --> how the artifacts generated by a DSR study relate to practice
		1. Requirements to which an artifact must conform
		2. Properties constraining the artifact's identity
		3. Component put together  to materialize the artifact
	3. Way of designing --> describe the design of the DSR artifact (how it is realized)
		1. Artifact representation (combining text and other visual elements)
		2. Design process (the development of the artifact)
		3. Implementation (in specific contexts)
		4. Use
		5. Evaluation

Then they classified several Conceptual Frameworks along these dimensions.

For my Conceptual Framework, I believe it falls into the answering phase, we want to convey all my experience and insights of building OCED. I'm not sure whether it falls into the generalized view or specific view, because OCED is a specific solution, but we want to take a generic view on OCED. Then lastly, for the research dimension, we're focusing on way of designing and then mostly artifact representation (how should OCED look like and what are the important concepts) and design process (given these concepts, how to develop OCED), implementation (although with a lesser focus).
